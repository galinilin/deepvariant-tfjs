DeepVariant v1.8, running in your browser via tensorflow.js.

* clicking this pulls a ~22 MB quantized checkpoint and warms up the WebGL backend on your GPU.

Yep — this really runs Google's DeepVariant 1.8 WGS model end-to-end in your browser. The candidate engine, the seven-channel pileup encoder, and the InceptionV3 network itself are all here, in TypeScript + tfjs. No server. Nothing leaves your machine.

I quantized the released checkpoint from float32 down to uint8 to shrink it from ~85 MB to ~22 MB. The conversion lives in galinilin/dv-tfjs and the parity tests confirm the predictions match upstream within rounding noise. Inference runs on your GPU through tfjs's WebGL backend.

Why does this exist?

I have been doing deep learning since 2017, and over the last year or so I have been gravitating toward the place where ML meets biology. DeepVariant felt like the cleanest thing to start from: a CNN doing real, useful work, used in real clinical pipelines, with a paper trail you can sit down and read. I wanted to take it apart instead of just calling .predict() on a black box, so I rebuilt the encoder and a tiny visualization layer around it. That turned into this sandbox.

If you want the canonical "how does DeepVariant see?" introduction, the Google AI blog post is the right starting point: Looking Through DeepVariant's Eyes. Upstream project: google/deepvariant.
